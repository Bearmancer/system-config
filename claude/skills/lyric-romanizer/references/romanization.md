# Romanization schemes (ASCII only, no diacritics)

Goal: someone who reads only English letters should be able to sing the line close to how it
sounds. So romanize **what is sung**, not letter by letter, and express every sound distinction
with ordinary letters (doubling, digraphs, one capital-letter convention) instead of accents.

Contents: Hindustani (Urdu / Hindi) · Punjabi and other Indic · Persian · Arabic · Japanese ·
Korean · Mandarin · Russian · Everything else

---

## Hindustani (Urdu script, Devanagari)

The scheme is shared by Urdu and Hindi so that the same song looks the same in either script.

**Vowels**

| Sound | Write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| short a (अ) | a | dil, kal |
| long aa (आ, ا) | aa | pyaar, raat |
| short i | i | dil, kisi |
| long ee (ई, ی) | ee | teer, meer, neend |
| short u | u | tum, kuchh |
| long oo (ऊ, و) | oo | hoon, sooraj |
| e (ए, ے) | e | tere, kehna |
| ai (ऐ) | ai | hai, main (I) |
| o (ओ) | o | roz, mohabbat |
| au (औ) | au | aur, kaun |

Rule of thumb for long vowels: double them (`aa`, `ee`, `oo`) when the sung vowel is clearly
long; keep single letters for short or unstressed ones. Be consistent within a song.

**Nasal vowels**: add a trailing `n` — hoon, kahaan, yahaan, nahin, aankhon. Keep the
spoken pair apart: `main` = मैं/میں "I", `mein` = में/میں "in".

**Consonants**

- Aspirates are letter + h: kh gh ch jh th dh ph bh (and retroflex Th Dh below).
- **Retroflex sounds use capital letters**: T Th D Dh R Rh (ट ठ ड ढ ड़ ढ़; ٹ ڈ ڑ).
  Dental t d stay lowercase. Example: baRa, ghaR, dhaDkan, peeTh.
- Because a capital letter always means "retroflex", write **everything else in lowercase**,
  including the first letter of each line and proper names (ram, dilli).
- sh (ش ष), z (ز ذ ض ظ ज़), j (ج ज), f (ف फ़), q (ق क़), kh/gh also for خ/غ (ख़/ग़).
- ع (ain) and ء (hamza) are not written; just keep the vowel: ishq, aalam, maani.
- ں (noon ghunna) and anusvara: trailing/medial `n` or `m` by place — kangan, sambhaal, manzil.
- w vs v follows the source script: و -> `w` (waqt, wafa), व -> `v` (vaqt, vaada).
- Aim for sung schwa deletion: kamal, not kamala; samjhaa, not samajhaa.

**Compounds**: izafat -> `-e-`  (shab-e-firaaq, dil-e-naadaan); conjunction و -> `-o-`
(naam-o-nishaan). Hyphenate only these, plus real compounds that sing as one word.

## Punjabi and other Indic scripts (Gurmukhi, Shahmukhi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati...)

Use the Hindustani scheme above. Tamil: ழ -> `zh`, ற -> `R`, ள -> `L`, ண -> `N`,
ன -> `n`. Bengali and similar: write the sung vowel (the inherent "o" sound is `o`, not `a`).
Tone and length contrasts that have no letter-based equivalent are left out.

## Persian

aa (آ ا), i or ee (long ی), u or oo (long و), a/e/o for short vowels. v for و as a consonant,
ch چ, zh ژ, kh خ, gh ق and غ, sh ش, s (ث ص س), z (ذ ض ظ ز), t (ط ت), h (ح ه), ع and ء dropped.

## Arabic

aa ee oo for long vowels. Keep the Arabic distinctions that English letters can show:
th (ث), dh (ذ), kh (خ), gh (غ), sh (ش), q (ق), h for both ح and ه. Emphatic consonants
(ص ض ط ظ) are written as plain s d t z. ع and ء are dropped. Sun-letter assimilation is
written as sung (ash-shams, not al-shams). Dialect: follow the singer's pronunciation, not
Modern Standard Arabic.

## Japanese

Hepburn without macrons: long vowels are doubled (ō -> `oo`, ū -> `uu`, ā -> `aa`;
`ei` stays `ei`). Particles as pronounced: は -> `wa`, へ -> `e`, を -> `o`.

## Korean

Revised Romanization (already ASCII) following pronunciation, including liaison and sound
changes: eo, eu, ae, oe, ss, kk. No hyphens inside words.

## Mandarin

Pinyin with no tone marks or numbers. Write ü as `yu` after n/l (nyu, lyu); after j/q/x the
plain `u` already means /y/ (ju, qu, xu).

## Russian

zh, kh, ts, ch, sh, shch, yu, ya, yo, y for ы and й, e for э; soft and hard signs dropped.

## Everything else

Use the standard simplified, diacritic-free system for that language (Hebrew academic-simple,
Thai RTGS, Greek ELOT-simple, Turkish and similar Latin-script languages are left as written).
Pick conventions a non-speaker can read aloud, and keep them consistent through the song.

---

## Lyrics already in Latin script

Leave them as written. Do not respell Roman-Urdu or other informal spellings. The only change:
if the text uses scholarly diacritics (macrons, dots under letters such as ā ī ū ṭ ḍ ṣ), convert
those to this scheme so the output is diacritic-free. Native orthographic accents of Latin-script
languages (Spanish, French, Turkish...) are spelling, not transliteration, and stay.
