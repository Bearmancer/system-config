Cast & relationship map — as of chapter 12

Coverage: every player introduced through chapter 12 · diagram v6: one colour per relation, solid lines only, no edge crosses a name box · names are canonical (see glossary)

How to read the colours

Each kind of relationship has its own colour — that is the only difference between lines: every line is solid and the same weight. The colours are this diagram's own key, defined by the legend below (not a universal scheme; they stay stable across chapters of this same map). A link the video's own fact-check flagged as disputed keeps a colour of its own rather than a different line style. The legend below the diagram is complete for this map, and every line is labelled in place.

Legend

  • Green — founding & ownership. Who created, bankrolled or co-created the company: DeVos and Van Andel → Amway; Patrick → Holiday Magic and Leadership Dynamics (plus his stake in Mind Dynamics); Everett → Mind Dynamics; Turner → Koscot and Dare to Be Great; Erhard → EST; Bakker → the PTL ministry at Charlotte 1974.
  • Blue — Amway sponsorship, as the video and the distributor/litigation traces support it — Renard's identity resolved via Wead's 2009 blog post; Conigliaro is a name only. Upline → downline: Yager → Renard; Yager → Conigliaro → Britt.
  • Grey — a link the video asserts but the record disputes. Hanson's sponsorship: the video says DeVos, the obituary record says the Basses (see lesson 02).
  • Purple — the video's account: the tape business. Renard's duplication feeding Yager's downline; Britt manufacturing and supplying tapes wholesale; Hall's late-1982 bid to build a rival tools line by crosslining (the attempt, not a success); Harteis's spin-off of Yager's line (ch11).
  • Brown — the Fontainebleau proposal. One pitch, two answers: declined by Yager, accepted by Britt (outcomes in the labels).
  • Amber — intellectual lineage (the video's account). Hill is said to have inspired Nightingale → the duplication practice; Carnegie and Peale are the same self-help wave.
  • Magenta — family. The household links the chapters give: Birdie Yager; Jim and Tammy Faye Bakker (ch11).
  • Teal — testimony & authorship. Who tells the story and who wrote it: Wead is the source of the Fontainebleau founding account; Wead was Yager's ghostwriter on the books pushed as tools (ch6); Wead co-wrote the 1979 Peoples Temple book with Phil Kerns; Eric Scheibeler and Ruth Carter later corroborate the same fake-it thesis Postma states (ch8); DeVos gives the sworn surrender Q&A in the Setzer case, April 1988 (ch10).
  • Red — separation & termination. Amway Corporation and the tools businesses: different owners, "historically acrimonious" (ch3); Amway terminates Leonard Hall's distributorship for crosslining, late 1982 (the video's account — now with one critic-hosted trace consistent with it, the archived Amquix attorneys page); the Bakkers' mid-1970s split from Robertson's CBN (ch11).
  • Navy — regulatory enforcement. The FTC moves against all three pyramid sellers: Holiday Magic (Docket 8834 — with state and SEC suits alongside), Koscot (Docket 8888, order 1975), Amway (complaint 1975, decision 1979) — plus the September 2026 return: the FTC with Washington State filed against Amway, World Wide Group and Leadership Team Development (proposed $225m settlement, W.D. Wash. 2:26-cv-03474), the first FTC action at the Approved Provider/BSM training system. Filed and proposed, not a verdict.
  • Olive — political & ideological ties. DeVos and Van Andel's private 1975 White House visit to Ford (the visit is the video's assertion; no record of its content located — but the Ford papers do hold Amway-related material: 1965 convention speech, Aug 1975 White House press file; the Daily Diaries are the unsearched-by-us place a visit would appear); Ford speaking at Amway conventions; Yager bringing candidate Reagan to the 1980 Charlotte rally; Wead as adviser and conduit to the Bush family; Patrick's membership in the John Birch Society; Erhard's involvement in Mind Dynamics; Yager's FGBMFI membership (thesis-supported); Bakker's PTL board seat for Yager and the 1974 Charlotte founding; Falwell's Moral Majority galvanising toward Reagan 1980 (the "1980 boost" is the video's inference).
  • Violet — parallel-model resemblance. The same pyramid-shaped MLM form shared by Amway, Holiday Magic, Koscot and Dare to Be Great — with no personal link claimed between those companies and this cast (the Koscot-distributors-turned-kingpins crossover is tape lore only; see "not drawn").
  • Copper — internal discipline / corporate warnings. Amway policing its own distributors: the Postma memo (Postma → Sullivan, 10 Jan 1983); the Directly Speaking tapes (spoken by Rich DeVos alone per the court-exhibit transcripts — the video says "DeVos and Van Andel"); executive visits to the kingpins (the video's account; dates unsourced).
  • Charcoal — the confrontation. The Miami Showdown, Fontainebleau Hotel terrace, 5 April 1983: Amway's men (Lindebom, Abraham) face the kingpins — one link from the corporate men to the kingpin group at the terrace, one link between the two corporate men (the tape's account — no independent record of the meeting; names unverified).
  • Rose — media scrutiny. The press closing in as two separate items with no causal link between them: CBS 60 Minutes "Soap and Hope" (Mike Wallace interviews the Yagers, January 1983) and Kerns' book (1982) — the video's inference is broadcast+book+Hall spooking the suits.

Who is who — the roster

The company side

  • Rich DeVos & Jay Van Andel — Amway's co-founders (1959). DeVos also has a walk-on in this story: the video says he sponsored Fred Hanson in the '50s; the record shows the Basses did. In 1975 both men privately visit President Ford in the White House — the visit is the video's assertion; what was said there is unknown (ch7).
  • Amway Corporation — the soap-and-products company; not the tools business (separate owners, separate profits — ch3's two-pyramid distinction). Fights the FTC 1975–79 and survives with safe-harbor conditions (the video's phrase; not a statutory safe harbor) the suits read as a draw, not an absolution (ch7).
  • The FTC (ch7) — the regulator that killed Holiday Magic and Koscot and then came for "the biggest steer on the ranch": complaint against Amway March 1975, final order May 1979 (In re Amway Corp., 93 F.T.C. 618).
  • Ed Postma (ch8) — Amway lawyer, head of the business-conduct division; author of the 10 January 1983 "Analysis of the Britt/Yager System" memo to Patric Sullivan. His verdict, direct quote: "The tool business is illegal. Although the Amway business is legal, the tools business is not. It is a pyramid."
  • Patric Sullivan (ch8) — Amway director of sales, Northwest region; the Postma memo's recipient. (The video's captions say "Patrick"; the memo spells it "Patric".)
  • Ron Lindebom (ch9) — Amway director of sales, western region; the corporation's spokesman at the Miami Showdown alongside Bill (William) Abraham, delivering the lawyer-drafted "reasonably related to the sale of the products to the ultimate consumer" directive. Flag: the captions garble the name ("Linde Baum", "Lindbom") and the record could not confirm it — treat as the video's account.
  • Bill (William) Abraham (ch9) — outside counsel, not an Amway company lawyer: William J. Abraham, Abraham Law Offices, Columbus, Ohio — a long-time representative of distributors, brought in for this meeting (the SHS pleading says Amway "brought or permitted its outside counsel… to talk with Amway Diamonds"); later filings (Morrison v. Amway, 49 F. Supp. 2d 529; P&G v. Amway, 80 F. Supp. 2d 639) show him as counsel for the Yager/distributor side. Flag: the captions render the name "Bob A-" / "Bobby Abraham" — that is wrong.

The chain that matters

  • Dexter Yager — joined Amway 1964; the tape business's distribution half. Born 1939 in Fulton, New York (raised in Rome); car salesman and a salesman for West End Brewery's Utica Club; moved to Charlotte 1969 with Birdie and the children. Sponsors Renard (1966 — oral tradition only); declines the $50,000 tape-plant pitch; distributes Britt's tapes through his downlines (the video's account). Brings candidate Reagan to the 1980 Charlotte Amway rally (ch6) (the video's account).
  • Fred Hanson — "one of the original Amway distributors"; possibly the man who lent Yager the Nightingale record (unverified either way).
  • Tony Renard — sponsored by Yager, Fall 1966; buys a second-hand tape recorder from him and starts duplicating motivational tapes; by the late '60s they move through Yager's downline — "at first, they're not a profit center". This is the practice Britt later scales into mass manufacturing; the chapters record no direct link between Renard and Britt (the video's founding trio is Britt, Yager, and the nameless man). Caveat: the name is unfindable in the accessible record — treat as oral tradition (lesson 02).
  • Dominic Conigliaro — Yager's downline; sponsors Bill Britt into Amway in 1970. Name spelling varies in the record (Coniguliaro/Congilero).
  • Bill Britt — the tape business's manufacturing half. Army 1949, Korean War officer; municipal management in central North Carolina (the video says "Charlotte area"; the record says Raleigh, Sanford, Goldsboro, Carrboro); joined Amway 1970. At the Fontainebleau coffee shop he answered the nameless man's pitch with the video's rendering, "I'll do it" (true source: Wead's 2009 blog post).
  • ? The nameless young man — the pitch's originator, unnamed in the chapter's source; identity genuinely unknown (Wead considered and set aside).
  • Leonard Hall (ch7) — a West Coast distributor who "goes rogue" late 1982: seeing the tools money, he poaches distributors up and down the California coast to build a rival tools line of his own — crosslining, "the ultimate sin in the Amway universe" — and Amway terminates his distributorship. The video admits it knows very little about Hall; one critic-hosted trace is consistent with the video's account — the archived Amquix attorneys page records "a crown distributorship, Leonard Hall in California was selling his system to the distributors who had no system at the time… in the early 1980's". No press or court trace surfaced.

Around the founders

  • Birdie Yager — Dexter's wife; four children at the Charlotte move, seven eventually.
  • Doug Wead — tools kingpin and the chapter's key source (the Fontainebleau account is his). Chapter 6 profiles him: born Indiana 1946 (the video's dating — unchecked); Yager's assistant, press agent and ghostwriter (the Reagan biography he wrote is dedicated to Yager); wired into the Republican establishment through Amway; close to the Bush family under both 41 and 43, then White House staff 1989–90; released secretly taped Bush conversations in 2005; later advised the Pauls. Chameleon source — read with the spin in mind.
  • Betty Jo Renfrow — wife of tools kingpin Rex Renfrow; the "tape of the day, or the tape of the hour" line (ch3). Spelling corrected per the record (captions render "Betty Joe Renfro").
  • Phil Kerns (ch7) — former distributor; author of Fake It 'Til You Make It (1982), called by a critic the first book-length exposé of Amway and the tools cult. Irony of record: he once co-wrote a book about the Peoples Temple with Doug Wead (Peoples Temple, Peoples Tomb, 1979). The January 1983 60 Minutes exposé "Soap and Hope" (CBS — Mike Wallace interviews Dexter and Birdie Yager, goes "fairly easy" on them, leaves the tools-vs-Amway income question open) — the video reads the two together as the press heat that spooked the suits into action.
  • Jerry "Gerry" Herrera (ch9) — tools kingpin who "controlled much of the tools business in Puerto Rico"; present at the Miami Showdown, mapped alongside the kingpins. Lead only, unverified: distributor listings render the first name "Gerry" — treat as the video's account.
  • Ron Hale (ch9) — tools kingpin, a Direct directly downline of Dexter Yager; present at the Miami Showdown, mapped alongside the kingpins. Unfindable in the record — treat as the video's account.
  • Eric Scheibeler & Ruth Carter (ch8) — later corroborating critics, rostered here ahead of their books (which belong to later chapters): Scheibeler (former federal auditor turned "Founder's Emerald", Merchants of Deception — per his book) and Carter (pseudonym; 15 years a distributor, Behind the Smoke and Mirrors, 1999 — per her book) both corroborate Postma on the fake-it display economy.

The parallel empires (ch5)

  • William Penn Patrick — founded Holiday Magic Inc. (1964) on the Amway multilevel model; founded Leadership Dynamics (1967), pioneering large-group awareness training; held a financial stake in Mind Dynamics; member of the John Birch Society (the video's account — no record source listed); died 1973; the companies ended amid state, SEC and FTC actions and the 1974 order.
  • Alexander Everett — Patrick's mentor; founder of Mind Dynamics, the "sister corporation" of Holiday Magic.
  • Glenn W. Turner — Holiday Magic employee turned founder: Koscot Interplanetary (cosmetics, like Holiday Magic) and Dare to Be Great! (1969), graduated self-motivation courses on cassette, $8,000 for all four levels. Koscot was driven out by the FTC in 1975; Turner went to prison in 1987 for a later pyramid scheme and died 2020.
  • Holiday Magic Inc. — Patrick's cosmetics MLM; prosecuted by California, the SEC and the FTC as an illegal pyramid (FTC Docket 8834).
  • Koscot Interplanetary — Turner's cosmetics MLM; the name joins "Kos" (cosmetics) + "cot" (communities of tomorrow) + "interplanetary" (never an Epcot reference) (FTC Docket 8888, order 1975). Celebrity footnote: F. Lee Bailey was indicted with Turner on 18 May 1973 (28 counts mail fraud + conspiracy; trial from 17 Sept 1973; Bailey severed 25 Apr 1974; mistrial 30 May 1974, jury could not agree; charges then dropped against Bailey) — verified (United States v. Bailey, M.D. Fla. 1975).
  • Leadership Dynamics — Patrick's 1967 large-group-awareness company.
  • Mind Dynamics — Everett's company, Patrick's stake, Erhard's involvement; subject of The Pit (Church & Carnes, 1972).
  • Dare to Be Great! — Turner's 1969 Orlando motivation-course company; the cassette-and-seminar model the chapter holds up as the tools' forerunner.
  • Werner Erhard & EST — Erhard was involved in Mind Dynamics, then founded Erhard Seminars Training (EST); the human-potential movement's most famous brand, name-checked from The Wiz to Diana Ross fandom.
  • John Birch Society — the far-right "worldwide communist conspiracy" organization; Patrick was a member (the video's account — no record source listed). The chapter's bridge from motivational cults to far-right politics (with the kingpins' conservative-evangelical-Republican profile flagged as a forward thread).

The politicians (ch6–ch7)

  • Ronald Reagan (ch6) — candidate Reagan speaks at Yager's massive 1980 Charlotte Amway rally (the video's account) (Wead wrote the campaign biography Reagan in Pursuit of the Presidency, dedicated to Yager).
  • The Bush family (ch6) — George H. W. Bush (41) and George W. Bush (43): Wead becomes close to the family, serves as religious-right conduit in the White House 1989–90, is cashiered after the 1990 hate-crimes-bill fight (cause as the video tells it), and burns the bridge by releasing secretly taped calls in 2005.
  • Gerald Ford (ch7) — Grand Rapids congressman next door to Ada, accidental president; Amway's "secret weapon" that Holiday and Koscot lacked. DeVos and Van Andel visit him privately in the White House in 1975 — asserted, content unknown, no record of the conversation located; the Ford papers do hold Amway-related material (1965 convention speech; Aug 1975 White House press file) and the Daily Diaries are the unsearched-by-us place a visit would appear. A fixture at Amway conventions thereafter.

The surrender & the afterlife (ch10–ch12)

  • Setzer v. Amway (ch10) — Rick Setzer and Sue Lynn Setzer v. Amway Corp., Richard M. DeVos and Jay Van Andel, filed 17 July 1986 (Greenville, SC), tried April 1988; plaintiff Rick Setzer a Triple Diamond suing over interference with his tools business; judgment $160,938.72. DeVos's sworn Q&A ("those abuses continued to this day"; "upward of $300 million in volume"; "never pursued it to its ultimate goal of really nailing anything down") is the video's surrender, verbatim from the critic-hosted transcript excerpt.
  • The Ten Points (ch10) — the January 1983 pledges from the Directly Speaking tape (Diamond Bulletin, February 1983); the "10 points" DeVos is asked about on the stand. A document, not a node.
  • FGBMFI (Full Gospel Business Men's Fellowship International) (ch11) — the Pentecostal businessmen's network (incorporated January 1952); the video's bridge between the tools cult and televangelism ("essentially built prosperity gospel" is the video's gloss).
  • Oral Roberts (ch11) — Pentecostal televangelist; a founding figure of FGBMFI.
  • John Osteen (ch11) — Houston pastor with early FGBMFI affiliation per scholarship; Joel Osteen connects by lineage only.
  • Jim Bakker & Tammy Faye Bakker (ch11) — televangelists; fall out with Robertson's CBN, move to Charlotte 1974, found the PTL ministry with Yager on its board; Bakker resigns 19 March 1987 over the payoff to Jessica Hahn, is convicted 1989 and paroled 1994.
  • PTL ministry (ch11) — the Bakkers' Charlotte ministry from 1974.
  • Pat Robertson (ch11) — head of the Christian Broadcasting Network (hired Bakker 1966); the mid-1970s falling-out sends the Bakkers to Charlotte.
  • Jerry Falwell (ch11) — founder of the Moral Majority (June 1979); the chapter's marker for the Christian right's galvanising toward Reagan 1980.
  • Fred Harteis (ch11) — head of a spin-off of Yager's line; his organisation's 5,000-strong seminars are where Yager told the Hillary Clinton story (the figure attaches to the seminars generally; advocacy-sourced). Spelling: Harteis — the captions say "Hartis". Named again in the side video as a Yager-line kingpin.
  • WWG + LTD (ch12) — World Wide Group and Leadership Team Development, co-defendants with Amway in the September 2026 filing.
  • FTC + Washington State, September 2026 (ch12) — filed 17 September 2026 against Amway, WWG and LTD; proposed $225m settlement (W.D. Wash. 2:26-cv-03474); the first FTC action at the Approved Provider/BSM training system. Filed and proposed — allegations, no final judgment; postdates the video.
  • The departed (ch12) — Bill Britt (23 January 2013, Ponte Vedra Beach); Doug Wead (10 December 2021); Jay Van Andel (7 December 2004, Ada); Rich DeVos (6 September 2018, Ada); Dexter Yager (6 January 2019, Charlotte). Endpoints, not nodes: no new relations to draw.
  • Shaun Munger (ch12) — the video's maker and narrator; speaks but is not drawn — he stands outside the cluster.

The lineage (the map's lower band)

  • Earl Nightingale, "The Strangest Secret" (1956) — an early motivational record (the "first Gold Record" tag is the label's marketing) and the model for the duplication practice.
  • Napoleon Hill (Think and Grow Rich, 1937) — Nightingale's inspiration; the video's "con man" characterization is substantially supported by investigative journalism.
  • Dale Carnegie & Norman Vincent Peale — the same 1930s–50s wave the tools recycled.

Context that is deliberately not drawn

  • The Koscot-distributors-turned-kingpins crossover — several former Koscot distributors "apparently became tools cult kingpins"; the claim rests on an unlocated mention on the Miami tape (no timestamp available). Tape lore, not a drawable link: no names, no lines.
  • The White House deal — whether the 1975 Ford visit produced an explicit or implicit understanding about the FTC. The video itself refuses to pick ("we may never know"); the map draws the visit, not the deal.
  • The unverified numbers — the $30M-a-year Yager figure and the 2%/98% tools-profit split are the video's figures, not the record's (lesson 03); no line is annotated with them.
  • The Hawaii parallel — "cryptic references" to a second crosslining affair the video says it "couldn't nail down". Unnailed, undrawn.
  • Amway's own small motivational operation — exists at this date, separate from Britt/Yager's venture.
  • Walk-ons — Barry Goldwater (endorsed Wead's 1992 Democratic opponent), Ron and Rand Paul (Wead's later advisees), the January-6 / Iced Earth aside, QAnon, Scientology, F. Lee Bailey and O.J. Simpson, the Manson–Fromme montage figures, Diana Ross and The Wiz: colour in the treatises, not actors in this wiring.

  • The Directly Speaking correction — the January 1983 tapes were spoken by Rich DeVos alone per the court-exhibit transcripts (the video says "DeVos and Van Andel"); the copper legend row carries the correction.
  • Voices beyond the video's own cast — Jim Hussey (an Amway representative audible on the Miami tape) and Ron Puryear (named in the side video alongside Harteis as a Yager-line kingpin) come from the source material around the video, not the video's own account: context only, not drawn.
