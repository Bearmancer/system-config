Reference — who is who, and who is wired to whom

Cast & relationship map — as of chapter 14

Coverage: every named person the reader has met through chapter 14 (late January → 1 May 1935) · names follow the cast glossary, which is canonical for this workspace · companion lesson: Lesson 01 — chapter 14 · timeline: timeline

How to read this map

Colour is the only line difference. Every line is solid, the same weight, with a colour-matched arrowhead. What a colour means is defined only by the legend below — and a colour's meaning, once set, holds across this workspace's chapter subgraphs and this map, so the palette never needs re-learning. Labels on the lines name the relation; the roster spells every relation out in words, so nothing depends on colour vision alone.

Legend

  • Authority / promotion — appoints, promotes, hands a post or a chair.
  • Patronage — a patron raises a protégé (Kaganovich → Yezhov, Kaganovich → Khrushchev).
  • Plenum attack / denunciation — public accusation aimed at destroying a courtier (Yezhov, Beria and Sergo → Yenukidze).
  • Old friendship, souring — a personal tie from the revolutionary years now strained (Stalin → Yenukidze).
  • NKVD case collaboration — joint work extracting confessions (Yezhov → Yagoda → Zinoviev/Kamenev).
  • Household / marriage / memory — wives, salons, godparenthood, the Nadya toast (Yezhov → Feigenberg → salon; Stalin → Nadya; Yenukidze → Nadya).
  • Suspicion of foul play — a death read as possible murder (Kuibyshev → Yagoda: the poisoning claims).

Roster — everyone on the map, in words

Central

  • Stalin — promotes Yezhov and hands Kaganovich the railways; presides over the Plenum; closes the chapter with the Nadya toast. Old, souring friend of Yenukidze.
  • Nikolai Yezhov (ch14) — Kaganovich's protégé; promoted by Stalin to Kirov's CC Secretary chair and, 31 March, to NKVD oversight; runs the Kirov case; with Yagoda extracts confessions from Zinoviev and Kamenev; leads the Plenum attack on Yenukidze; marries Yevgenia Feigenberg.
  • Abel Yenukidze (ch14) — Nadya's godfather and Stalin's old friend; attacked at the Plenum by Yezhov, Beria and Sergo; expelled from the Central Committee and the Party, demoted to a Caucasian sanatorium.
  • Yevgenia Feigenberg (ch14) — Yezhov's second wife; keeps the literary salon (Babel, Utsesov, Pilniak).
  • Nikita Khrushchev (ch14) — Kaganovich's protégé; handed Moscow as First Secretary on 1 February; self-styled Stalin "son".
  • Lazar Kaganovich (ch14) — takes the railways 1 February; patron of Yezhov and Khrushchev; supplies the Plenum line for the record.
  • Genrikh Yagoda (ch14) — NKVD chief; works with Yezhov to extract confessions; presses Zinoviev and Kamenev; carries the suspicion over Kuibyshev's death; bruised by the Plenum exchanges.

Around them

  • Valerian Kuibyshev (ch14) — dies 25 January, opening the chapter; had sided with Kirov and Sergo against the NKVD's handling of the case; the poisoning claims point at Yagoda. His son Vladimir believed murder.
  • Sergo Ordzhonikidze (ch14) — Kuibyshev's ally; friend of both Yezhovs; joins the attack on Yenukidze at the Plenum. His wife Zina and daughter Eteri witness the Yezhov household.
  • Lavrenti Beria (ch14) — attacks Yenukidze at the Plenum; author of the falsified Transcaucasus book inscribed to Stalin.
  • Zinoviev and Kamenev (ch14) — pressed by Yagoda and Yezhov until they confess responsibility for Kirov's murder.
  • Yezhov salon (ch14) — the Babel–Utsesov–Pilniak circle around Yevgenia Feigenberg; the Mandelstams witnessed the Yezhov courtship at Sukhumi in 1930.
  • Lev Mekhlis (ch14) — Pravda editor; forwards Yenukidze's marked book to Stalin, supplying the marginalia ("That's false!", "fibs", "balderdash!") that catalyses the fall.
  • Anastas Mikoyan (ch14) — moved up to full Politburo member 1 February; overnight guest; rebukes Yenukidze at the Plenum ("You acted wrongly").
  • Kliment Voroshilov (ch14) — rebukes Yenukidze at the Plenum ("What are you? Some kind of child?").
  • Maria Svanidze (ch14) — denounces Yenukidze's "stench" to Stalin; the Kuntsevo exchange. Her husband Alyosha is "like a brother" to Stalin.
  • Mikhail Kalinin (ch14) — the president; receives a maid's appeal in the Nadya-gossip case.
  • Nikolai Bukharin (ch14) — on Yezhov: "good heart and clean conscience" — while noticing the grovelling before Stalin.
  • Nestor Lakoba (ch14) — published the sycophantic 1934 history of Stalin in Batumi, the counter-example to Yenukidze's under-praising book.

Past timeline — dead or offstage, but driving the chapter

  • Kirov — murdered December 1934; his case is run by Yezhov and his CC Secretary chair taken by Yezhov.
  • Nadya Alliluyeva — her death haunts the chapter: Yenukidze was her godfather and "swayed" her; a maid is arrested for gossiping about her suicide; Stalin's May-Day toast is to her. Her sister Zhenya is at the table; her children Svetlana and Vasily appear in the household footnotes.

Context that is deliberately not drawn

  • Offstage individuals (first wives, earlier husbands, memoir witnesses, office staff, the 110 arrested Kremlin employees, the "Countess", the dacha's builder) are named in the glossary and the lesson rather than as nodes: drawing every passing name would bury the court's working relations.
  • Information-flow links (Mekhlis forwarding the book; Kaganovich's "smelled a rat" line; the Stalin–Kaganovich letters behind the quotes) are described in the roster and the lesson: they are acts of paperwork, not court relations, and lines for them would cross the whole map.
  • Kirov's case and chair pass to Yezhov, but the arrow would cross the Stalin–Yenukidze friendship line's corridor; the roster states the transfer in words instead.
  • Parallels (Lakoba's and Beria's sycophant histories set against Yenukidze's book) are a literary comparison the chapter makes, not a relation between courtiers — prose, not lines.
