Timeline — as of chapter 14

Flashbacks the chapter relies on (1917–1934)

  • Feb 1917
    Khrushchev meets Kaganovich in Yuzovka during the February Revolution — the start of the patronage line. The book's account.
  • 1919
    Yezhov joins the Red Army; marries Antonina "Tonya" Titova. The book's account.
  • 1921–25
    Yezhov climbs: the Tartar Republic (1921), Kirgizia secretary (1925), then the Communist Academy and the CC. The book's account.
  • 1930
    Yezhov divorces Tonya; at Sukhumi the Mandelstams witness the courtship of the next wife. The book's account.
  • Nov 1930
    Yezhov's office meeting with Stalin; Politburo attendance follows. The book's account.
  • 1932
    Nadya Alliluyeva dies; Yenukidze, her godfather, is first to see the body. The book's account; the chapter treats the suicide as established and disputes only its political uses.
  • 1933
    Yezhov takes a role purging the Party. The book's account.
  • 1934
    Yezhov collapses in boils; treated with the best medical care in Central Europe, embassy dispatches tracking his health. The book's account.
  • 1934
    Lakoba publishes the sycophantic history of Stalin in Batumi; Beria inscribes his falsified Transcaucasus book "To my dear, adored master". The book's account.
  • 1 Dec 1934
    Kirov murdered — the case and the empty CC Secretary chair that drive this chapter. The book's account.

The chapter's own run (January → May 1935)

  • 25 Jan 1935
    Valerian Kuibyshev dies, aged 47. Officially heart disease and alcoholism; his son Vladimir believed murder; the book refuses to over-conclude. Date the book's account; cause disputed inside the chapter itself.
  • 1 Feb 1935
    Promotions: Kaganovich to the railways, Khrushchev to Moscow First Secretary; Mikoyan and Chubar to full Politburo members, Zhdanov and Eikhe to candidates. The book's account.
  • 31 Mar 1935
    Yezhov formally designated to supervise the NKVD; he already runs the Kirov case and holds Kirov's CC Secretary chair. The book's account.
  • Spring 1935
    The Kremlin Case: 110 of Yenukidze's employees arrested for terrorism; two sentenced to death, the rest to the camps. The book's account.
  • Spring 1935
    Yenukidze's Secret Bolshevik Printing Presses draws Stalin's marginalia ("That's false!", "fibs", "balderdash!") — the catalyst of the fall. The book's account.
  • Spring 1935
    Plenum: Yezhov and Beria accuse Yenukidze of sheltering terrorists; expelled from the Central Committee and the Party, sacked, made to publish a "Correction of Errors", demoted to a Caucasian sanatorium. The book's account.
  • 1 May 1935
    May-Day kebabs at Kuntsevo; the table toasts Nadya — "she crippled me". The book's account.
