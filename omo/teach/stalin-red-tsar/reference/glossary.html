Glossary — as of chapter 14

Coverage: every named person the reader has met through chapter 14 · no information from later chapters · lessons must use these names

Reader timeline — as of May 1935

Each entry states only the person's role and actions up to the end of chapter 14. Where the book itself teases later developments, that tease is left out. Names in italics are the aliases the court actually used.

Central

  • Stalin (Iosif Vissarionovich) — promotes, punishes, presides; the Nadya toast closes the chapter. Josef, Vozhd, Koba.
  • Nikolai Yezhov — the "Dwarf": new CC Secretary (Kirov's chair), designated 31 March to supervise the NKVD; Kaganovich's protégé, newly embraced into Stalin's circle. Kolya; Stalin's "blackberry" (yezhevika).
  • Abel Yenukidze — the "Casanova": Nadya's godfather, an old sybaritic friend of Stalin's; sacked, demoted, expelled from the Party in this chapter. Uncle Abel.
  • Yevgenia Feigenberg — Yezhov's second wife; flamboyant literary hostess of the Babel–Utsesov circle.
  • Nikita Khrushchev — new Moscow First Secretary; Kaganovich's protégé; self-styled Stalin "son".
  • Lazar Kaganovich — takes over the railways; patron of both Khrushchev and Yezhov; "at once smelled a rat".
  • Genrikh Yagoda — NKVD chief; works with Yezhov to extract confessions; bruised by the Plenum exchanges.

Around them

  • Valerian Kuibyshev — dies 25 January 1935, opening the chapter; his son Vladimir believed murder.
  • Sergo (Ordzhonikidze) — Kuibyshev's ally, friend of both Yezhovs; Plenum line on charity: "So what if she starves to death…".
  • Zina — Sergo's wife; Eteri — their daughter, witness to Yevgenia's wardrobe.
  • Anastas Mikoyan — moved up to full Politburo member; overnight guest; the Lipki trip.
  • Vlas Chubar — also moved up to full Politburo member (chapter footnote).
  • Andrei Zhdanov — candidate member; 1934 holiday companion of Stalin and Kirov.
  • Robert Eikhe — candidate member, West Siberia boss (footnote).
  • Kliment Voroshilov — Plenum: "What are you? Some kind of child?". Klim.
  • Nikolai Bukharin — on Yezhov: "good heart and clean conscience" (and notes the grovelling).
  • Mikhail Kalinin — the president; receives a maid's appeal in the Nadya-gossip case.
  • Nestor Lakoba — published the sycophantic 1934 history of Stalin in Batumi.
  • Lavrenti Beria — falsified his Transcaucasus book ("To my dear, adored master…"); attacks Yenukidze at the Plenum.
  • Lev Mekhlis — "weasel-faced" Pravda editor; forwarded the marked book to Stalin.
  • Maria Svanidze — denouncer of Yenukidze's "stench"; the Kuntsevo exchange.
  • Alyosha Svanidze — "like a brother" to Stalin; frequent overnight guest.
  • Zhenya (Alliluyeva) — Nadya's sister; at the May Day table.
  • Svetlana — asks for the Lipki dacha (chapter footnote); allowed to stay up for dinner.
  • Vasily — often dines with the adults (footnote).

Referenced only — past timeline

  • Kirov — his case and his chair drive the chapter; murdered eight weeks before it opens.
  • Nadya (Nadezhda Alliluyeva) — her death haunts the chapter; godfather, swaying, the toast.
  • Yasha (Yakov Dzhugashvili) — cited in Stalin's toast about shooting himself.
  • Zinoviev and Kamenev — forced to confess responsibility for Kirov's murder.
  • Osip Mandelstam (and wife) — sanatorium witnesses to the Yezhov courtship in 1930.
  • Antonina "Tonya" Titova — Yezhov's first wife, divorced 1930.
  • Isaac Babel, Leonid Utsesov, Boris Pilniak — the Yezhov salon.
  • Filipp Goloshchekin — Yezhov's sometime lover; organizer of the Romanov murder.
  • Khayutin and Gadun — Yevgenia's earlier husbands.
  • Poskrebyshev and Dvinsky — Stalin's office; the coded Berlin note on Yezhov's health.
  • Vera Trail — unpublished memoir witness to Yezhov's manner.
  • Trotsky, Dzerzhinsky, Lenin — the Sukhumi dacha footnote (1924).

Unnamed

  • The "Countess" of the alleged poison-page plot; the maid arrested for gossiping about Nadya; the Old Bolshevik courting Tonya; the 110 arrested Kremlin employees; the two condemned to death; the dacha's millionaire builder (footnote).
