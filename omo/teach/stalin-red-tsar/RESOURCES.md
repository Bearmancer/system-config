## Knowledge — documents of the period
- **Khlevniuk, R. W. Davies, Kosheleva, Rees, Rogovaya (eds), *Stalin i Kaganovich: Perepiska 1931–36*** (Moscow 2001) **[D]**
  The actual Stalin ↔ Kaganovich letters the chapters quote. Use for: seeing a quote in full context (Russian).
- **Lih, Naumov, Khlevniuk (eds), *Stalin's Letters to Molotov*** (Yale 1995) **[D]**
  The other main correspondence channel of these years. Use for: Stalin's own framing of the crisis, in his words.
- **Dimitrov, Georgi (Ivo Banac, ed.), *The Diary of Georgi Dimitrov 1933–49*** (2003) **[D]**
  A Comintern insider's dated diary. *Caution: runs past your position.* Use for: dated entries near the current chapter only.

## Knowledge — memoir / self-account (all span decades: stop where the book leaves you)
- **Khrushchev, *Khrushchev Remembers*** (1971/74) **[M]** — the courtier's testimony; self-serving, cross-check everything.
- **Molotov (Felix Chuev, ed.), *Molotov Remembers*** (1993) **[M]** — the loyal deputy's own account.
- **Kaganovich (Felix Chuev, ed.), *Tak Govoril Kaganovich*** (1992) **[M]** — the patron's version.
- **Mikoyan, *Tak bylo*** (2000) **[M]** — the survivor's version.
- **Mikoyan, S., *Memoirs of Military Test-Flying and Life with the Kremlin's Elite*** (1999) **[M]** — the elite family view.
- **Alliluyeva, Svetlana, *Twenty Letters to a Friend*** (1967) **[M]** — the daughter's account.
- **Mandelstam, Nadezhda, *Hope Against Hope*** (1999 ed.) **[M]** — the literary side of the 1930s; witness to what the famine and the cases did to a family.
- **Larina, Anna, *This I Cannot Forget*** (1993) **[M]** — Bukharin's widow on the court's private life.

## Knowledge — scholarship
- **Khlevniuk, Oleg, *In Stalin's Shadow: the Career of Sergo Ordzhonikidze*** (1993) **[S]**
  Sergo is central to these chapters; the standard archival study. *Caution: runs past your position.*
- **Conquest, Robert, *Stalin and the Kirov Murder*** (1989) **[S]** and **Knight, Amy, *Who Killed Kirov?*** (1999) **[S]**
  The two positions on the case hanging over the current chapters. *Caution: both continue past your position.*
- **Fitzpatrick, Sheila, *Everyday Stalinism*** (1999) **[S]**
  Ordinary life under the system — useful when a chapter's courtyard detail needs context. *Caution: 1930s to 1941.*
- **Rees, E. A., *Stalinism and Soviet Rail Transport 1928–1941*** (1995) **[S]**
  Why the railways matter and what the job Kaganovich takes on actually was. *Caution: runs to 1941.*

## Withheld (spoiler rule)
Works that narrate periods beyond the reader's position — the mid-1930s security cases in their later phases, the trials, the war, the aftermath — are **not listed yet**. They are added chapter by chapter as the reading advances. This is deliberate: a bibliography can spoil a narrative, so this one lags the reader on purpose.

## Wisdom (Communities)

  Heavily moderated, answers carry citations. **Spoiler warning: browsing it will surface later events.** Use for: one targeted question, not browsing.
- [H-Russia (H-Net)](https://networks.h-net.org/h-russia)
  Academic network for Russian/Soviet history; book reviews and queries. Same spoiler warning: post a specific question rather than reading through.

## Gaps
- No English-language book-length study is dedicated to the Kremlin household in exactly 1934–36; the period is covered inside biographies (Montefiore's own notes are the practical index) and Russian-language document collections.
- Court testimony (memoirs) is abundant but self-serving; where a claim rests on one memoir, lessons must say so.
