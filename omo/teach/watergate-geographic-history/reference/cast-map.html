Cast roster — as of chapter 12

Covers chapters 5–12 · canonical names per glossary · per-chapter ties live in the lessons

How to read

Colour carries line meaning in chapter subgraphs; this page spells each tie in words, including for colour-vision limits. Each entry carries its introduction chapter (absolute) plus a link to the lesson holding the full role line. Chapter introduction marks are absolute.

White House and campaign

  • Richard Nixon (ch5) — candidate ordering the 1968 stall, president ordering the unit, the Brookings job, and the FBI deflection; resigns August 9, 1974; pardoned September 8; exile to Yorba Linda. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • H. R. Haldeman (ch5) — logs the stall order; relays the Brookings order; receives the June 19 hush-money instruction; carries the June 23 deflection; interlocutor on both June reels; resigns April 30, 1973. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • John Ehrlichman (ch5/6) — builds the unit, tasks Krogh; interlocutor on the June 20 reel; resigns April 30, 1973; serves time, writes spy novels. Full entry: Lesson 02.
  • Henry Kissinger (ch5) — feeds Paris intel to the campaign; lends Young; presented as leading leaker; secretary of state; resignation-letter recipient. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • John Mitchell (ch5) — in the Chennault loop; attorney general turned CREEP head weighing Liddy schemes; backs Hoover's Huston veto; serves 19 months at Maxwell, last freed. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • Tom Charles Huston (ch5) — assigned the 1970 bombing-halt review; warns the evidence looks bad; drafts the surveillance plan; names the Brookings file. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • Charles Colson (ch6) — pushes the fire-crew scheme; directs Hunt's Ellsberg work; serves seven months; founds a prison ministry. Full entry: Lesson 02.
  • John Dean (ch6) — kills the fire scheme; warns of a cancer March 21, 1973; central witness; reduced sentence; memoirist. Full entry: Lesson 02.
  • Egil Krogh (ch6) — co-heads the unit; authorises Fielding; serves early. Full entry: Lesson 02.
  • David Young (ch6) — co-heads the unit; coins the Plumbers name. Full entry: Lesson 02.
  • J. Edgar Hoover (ch7) — objects to the Huston Plan, killing it. Full entry: Lesson 03.
  • Richard Helms (ch7) — CIA director; October 1971 Oval partner; lever against the FBI inquiry. Full entry: Lesson 03.
  • Alexander Butterfield (ch8) — minds the recorders; discloses the system July 13 closed-door and July 16 public. Full entry: Lesson 04.
  • Rose Mary Woods (ch8) — transcribes reels; pedal account for the erased stretch. Full entry: Lesson 04.
  • Alexander Haig (ch10) — chief of staff hearing Nixon vacillate between fighting and resigning. Full entry: Lesson 06.
  • J. Fred Buzhardt (ch10) — counsel ordered to examine the June 23 tape. Full entry: Lesson 06.
  • Manolo Sanchez (ch7) — valet on the May 9, 1970 Lincoln visit. Full entry: Lesson 03.

Operators, burglars, targets

  • E. Howard Hunt (ch6) — operator with Liddy; Bay of Pigs veteran; Fielding and Watergate burglaries; returns to spy writing. Full entry: Lesson 02.
  • G. Gordon Liddy (ch6) — operator; Watergate tactician; later radio provocateur; dies 2021. Full entry: Lesson 02.
  • Daniel Ellsberg (ch5) — study compiler turned leaker; Fielding patient; discrediting target. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • James McCord (ch7) — CREEP man and ex-CIA burglar; arrest links crew to the White House. Full entry: Lesson 03.
  • Anna Chennault (ch5) — secret cable between campaign and Saigon. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • Nguyen Van Thieu (ch5) — boycotts the talks at campaign urging (record: November 2, 1968). Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • Bui Diem (ch5) — ambassador; Chennault contact. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • Lyndon Johnson (ch5) — wiretaps the channel; calls it treason; confronts Nixon; stays silent. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • Robert McNamara (ch5) — commissions the study June 1967. Full entry: Lesson 01.
  • Jeffrey Miller (ch7) — Kent State student killed; marks the protest wave. Full entry: Lesson 03.

Investigators, prosecutors, Congress, courts

  • Archibald Cox (ch8) — subpoenas the reels; refuses the Stennis compromise; dismissed October 20, 1973. Full entry: Lesson 04.
  • Leon Jaworski (ch8) — renews the subpoenas November 1973; wins the tapes case. Full entry: Lesson 04.
  • Elliot Richardson (ch9) — refuses the firing order; resigns. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • William Ruckelshaus (ch9) — refuses the firing order; resigns. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • Robert Bork (ch9) — carries out the dismissal; names Jaworski. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • Peter Rodino (ch10) — sets the impeachment timetable; three articles late July. Full entry: Lesson 06.
  • Tip O'Neill (ch10) — presses Rodino for the clock. Full entry: Lesson 06.
  • Warren Burger (ch10) — reads the 8–0 tapes decision July 24, 1974. Full entry: Lesson 06.

Press, election, succession, aftermath

  • Bob Woodward (ch9) — meets the garage source; with Bernstein covers the money trail; co-authors the Final Days account. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • Carl Bernstein (ch10) — with Woodward, chronicler; co-author of the Final Days account. Full entry: Lesson 06.
  • W. Mark Felt (ch9) — FBI deputy chief; guides Woodward as Deep Throat. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • George McGovern (ch9) — 1972 nominee; holds only Massachusetts and DC. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • Thomas Eagleton (ch9) — first running mate; withdraws. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • Sargent Shriver (ch9) — replacement running mate. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • Spiro Agnew (ch9) — pleads no contest October 10, 1973; resigns. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • Gerald Ford (ch9) — confirmed vice president December 1973; sworn 38th president August 9, 1974; pardons Nixon September 8. Full entry: Lesson 05.
  • David Frost (ch11) — records nearly 29 hours in 1977 at Monarch Bay. Full entry: Lesson 07.
  • Sean Munger (ch12) — presenter; solo narration; states the connections thesis. Full entry: Lesson 08.
