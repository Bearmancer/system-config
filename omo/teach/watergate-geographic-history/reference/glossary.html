Reference — terms and names

Glossary

As of chapter 12 · covers chapters 5–12 · lessons must use these names

Position and coverage

Canonical spellings below are evidence-adjusted from the corrections passes across all eight lessons. Caption variants are noted once per entry. Lessons must use the canonical form. Source-vs-record notes sit inline where a check changed the wording.

People — White House and campaign

Richard Nixon — President 1969–1974; orders the stall, the unit, the Brookings job, the FBI deflection; resigns August 9, 1974. Record: resignation and pardon dates confirmed; late-days lore taught as the video telling.

H. R. Haldeman — Chief of staff; logs the 1968 stall order, relays the Brookings order, interlocutor on the June 20 and June 23 reels. Captions show Halddederman; canonical: Haldeman. Record: resigned April 30, 1973, not fired — the video wording is corrected in lessons.

John Ehrlichman — Domestic-affairs chief; builds the Special Investigations Unit. Captions show Erlickman, Errolman; canonical: Ehrlichman.

John Mitchell — Attorney general, then re-election chief; in the Chennault loop; serves 19 months at Maxwell. Record: sentence confirmed with second witness.

Charles Colson — Special counsel; pushes the Brookings fire-crew scheme, directs Hunt's Ellsberg work; serves seven months, founds a prison ministry. Captions show Coulson; canonical: Colson.

John Dean — White House counsel; kills the fire-engine scheme, warns of a cancer on the presidency March 21, 1973, later central witness and memoirist.

Egil Krogh — Co-head of the unit with David Young; authorises the Fielding operation; serves his sentence early. Captions show Agel Kro, Krue, Krogue; canonical: Krogh, universally "Bud" in record.

David Young — Co-head of the unit from Kissinger's staff; coins the Plumbers name.

Tom Charles Huston — Young staffer; authors the February 1970 Chennault memo and the domestic-surveillance plan. Record: February 25, 1970 memo text and June 25, 1970 delivery to chiefs confirmed.

Henry Kissinger — National-security adviser, later secretary of state; feeds Paris intel to the campaign, lends Young, exhibit one for leaks, recipient of the resignation letter.

Alexander Haig — White House chief of staff in the endgame; hears Nixon vacillate. Captions show Hey; canonical: Haig.

J. Fred Buzhardt — Counsel ordered to examine the June 23 tape. Captions show Buzart; canonical: Buzhardt.

Rose Mary Woods — Personal secretary; transcribes reels, gives the foot-pedal account of the erased stretch, disbelieved on the full gap. Captions vary Rosemary; canonical per record: Rose Mary Woods.

Alexander Butterfield — Deputy assistant; minds the recorders with the Secret Service; discloses the system July 13 closed-door and July 16 public. Record: taping ends with the public disclosure, not the closed session.

Manolo Sanchez — Nixon's valet; accompanies the May 9, 1970 Lincoln Memorial visit.

People — operators and burglars

E. Howard Hunt — Ex-CIA officer and novelist; co-leader, Fielding and Watergate operator; Bay of Pigs veteran. Record: Barker his aide under code name Eduardo confirmed.

G. Gordon Liddy — Ex-FBI/Treasury man; unit operator under CREEP cover, Watergate tactician; later talk-radio host, died March 30, 2021. Captions show Litty, Ly; canonical: Liddy. Record: memoir and broadcast details confirmed in substance; tape-as-decoy theory stays the video telling.

James McCord — Ex-CIA man and Committee to Re-elect the President employee; arrest links burglars to the White House. Record confirmed.

Bernard Barker, Eugenio Martínez, Frank Sturgis — Bay of Pigs-linked burglars; Barker, Martínez, Sturgis among the Watergate crew. Record: participation firm; Sturgis CIA-employment claims disputed.

Alfred Baldwin — Spotter across the street at the Howard Johnson's. Program detail stays the video telling.

Frank Wills — Guard who finds the re-taped latch and calls police. Record confirmed in substance.

Dr. Lewis Fielding — Ellsberg's psychiatrist; Beverly Hills office burglarized September 3, 1971, file not at the office. Record confirmed; cabinet now at the Smithsonian.

People — investigators, prosecutors, judges

Archibald Cox — First special prosecutor; subpoenas the reels, refuses the Stennis compromise, dismissed October 20, 1973. Captions show Archimold, Archable; canonical: Archibald Cox.

Leon Jaworski — Second special prosecutor from November 1, 1973; renews the subpoena list headed by the June 20 reel; wins the tapes case. Captions show Jorski; canonical: Jaworski.

Elliot Richardson — Attorney general; refuses the firing order; resigns October 20, 1973. Record confirmed.

William Ruckelshaus — Deputy attorney general; refuses the firing order; resigns October 20, 1973. Captions show Ruckles House; canonical: Ruckelshaus.

Robert Bork — Solicitor general; as acting head carries out the dismissal. Captions show Bourke; canonical: Bork. Record: 1987 Supreme Court nomination rejected 42–58 — the video compression is corrected in the lesson.

Warren Burger — Chief Justice, Nixon appointee; reads the unanimous July 24, 1974 tapes decision. Captions show Burer; canonical: Burger. Record: 8–0 with Rehnquist not participating.

Peter Rodino — House Judiciary chairman; sets the impeachment timetable. Captions show Redino; canonical: Rodino. Record: three articles adopted late July; committee report ran 528 pages.

Tip O'Neill — House majority leader pressing Rodino in June 1974; Speaker from January 1977. Captions show O'Neal; canonical: O'Neill.

People — press, opponents, foreign figures

Daniel Ellsberg — Rand analyst; helps compile the study, copies it from October 1969, shares it with Neil Sheehan in March 1971 (not February — corrected). Captions show Ellburg; canonical: Ellsberg.

Neil Sheehan — Reporter receiving the study; Times begins publishing June 13, 1971. Record: March 1971 handoff confirmed.

Bob Woodward, Carl Bernstein — Post reporters covering the money trail; later authors of the Final Days account. Record: lore line follow the money invented for film, never said in the garage.

W. Mark Felt — FBI deputy chief; the Deep Throat garage source, secret until 2005. Record confirmed.

George McGovern — 1972 Democratic nominee; holds only Massachusetts and DC. Record: 520–17 electoral tally confirmed.

Thomas Eagleton — First running mate; withdraws after hospitalization revelations; threat-to-stay claim stays allegation. Record: withdrawal and Shriver replacement confirmed.

Sargent Shriver — Replacement running mate. Captions show Shrivever; canonical: Shriver.

Spiro Agnew — Vice president; pleads no contest October 10, 1973 to 1967 tax evasion, resigns; probation and fine. Captions show Agnu; canonical: Agnew.

Gerald Ford — Michigan congressman from 1948; vice president December 1973 under the 25th Amendment; president August 9, 1974; pardons Nixon September 8, 1974; loses 1976; died December 2006. Record confirmed throughout.

Anna Chennault — Campaign fundraiser; secret cable between campaign and Saigon. Captions show Chinult; canonical: Chennault. Record: Hughes book and Nixon Library memo confirm the affair as backstory.

Nguyen Van Thieu — South Vietnamese president; boycotts the talks — chapter says October 30, 1968, record puts the defiant refusal November 2. Corrected in lessons.

Bui Diem — South Vietnamese ambassador in Washington; Chennault contact point.

Lyndon Johnson — Sitting president; wiretaps the back channel, calls it treason to Dirksen November 2, confronts Nixon November 3, stays silent. Record confirmed.

Robert McNamara — Outgoing defense secretary; commissions the study June 17, 1967. Record: 7,000 pages, 47 volumes, delivered January 15, 1969.

J. Edgar Hoover — FBI director; objects to the Huston Plan, killing it. Captions show Jay Edgar; canonical: J. Edgar Hoover.

Richard Helms — CIA director; October 1971 Oval Office partner, later the lever against the FBI inquiry. October 1971 excerpt stays source-account, unverified verbatim.

Leonid Brezhnev — Soviet leader; 1974 summit across Moscow, Minsk, Oreanda near Yalta June 29. Captions show Brev; canonical: Brezhnev.

David Frost — Television journalist; records nearly 29 hours at the Monarch Bay house in 1977; May broadcasts draw about 45 million. Record confirmed.

Sean Munger — Presenter; states the connections thesis and closes with channel apparatus. Apparatus links confirmed.

Jeffrey Miller — Twenty-year-old Kent State student killed May 1970. Record: four killed confirmed.

Garrett Graff — Modern synthesis author cited by the source. Captions show Graph; canonical: Graff.

Places and institutions

CREEP (Committee to Re-elect the President) — Campaign fund and cover at 1701 Pennsylvania Avenue NW; the money trail reaches it. Captions show Creep; canonical: CREEP.

Brookings Institution — Think tank holding the believed Chennault file; Nixon orders the safe blown June 17, 1971; Colson fire-crew scheme stopped by Dean. Record: order wording confirmed; ISA-report existence doubted by historians.

Key Biscayne — Nixon houses bought December 1968 with Rebozo/Abplanalp financing; June 17–18 whereabouts contested. Captions show Biscane; canonical: Key Biscayne.

La Casa Pacifica — San Clemente estate bought 1969; unit-creation setting, ruling landing place, exile endpoint, sold about 1979–1980. Captions show Lacassa; canonical: La Casa Pacifica.

Room 16, Executive Office Building — Plumbers working base. Record confirmed.

Duke Zeibert's — Power restaurant at Connecticut and L from 1950 to 1994. Captions show Zebert's; canonical: Zeibert's.

Watergate complex — Chennault residence, burglary target, fashionable address; site layers: canal terminus, gas works to 1947, Water Gate Inn, luxury complex from the mid-sixties. Record confirmed with second witnesses.

Maxwell (Montgomery, Alabama) — Minimum-security camp where Mitchell and Colson serve. Record confirmed.

Yorba Linda — Nixon birthplace and library grounds; burial site; library opened 1990. Captions show Yorb Belinda; canonical: Yorba Linda.

Saigon / Ho Chi Minh City — Embassy torn down 1998; final evacuation April 29–30, 1975; ladder-helicopter photo taken at the Pittman apartments, not the embassy — corrected. Captions show Ho Chi Min; canonical: Ho Chi Minh City.

Terms and practices

Chennault affair — 1968 back-channel stall of the Paris talks; the founding secret. Haldeman notes verbatim confirmed; boycott date corrected to November 2.

Pentagon Papers — The classified study, copied from October 1969, shared March 1971, published from June 13, 1971. Handoff month corrected.

Plumbers (Special Investigations Unit) — Covert leak-hunting unit formed within a week of publication, early July 1971; name from Young's leak joke and door sign. Record confirmed.

Huston Plan — Domestic-surveillance wish list to the chiefs June 25, 1970; Hoover veto shelves it; ideas recur as the Plumbers. Record confirmed.

Smoking-gun tape — June 23, 1972 Nixon–Haldeman conversation on blocking the FBI probe via the CIA; released August 5, 1974; ends congressional support. Record and transcript confirmed.

18½-minute gap — Erased stretch on the June 20 Nixon–Haldeman reel; buzz in place of speech; Woods pedal account disbelieved for the full length; maker and method open. Record: length and forensics confirmed; joint-taping theory taught as interpretation.

Deep Throat — Press name for the garage source, Felt, secret until 2005.

Saturday Night Massacre — October 20, 1973: Cox fired, Richardson and Ruckelshaus resign, Bork complies; Jaworski named November 1 with the same mandate. Record confirmed.

25th Amendment — Certified February 1967; first used to install Ford December 1973. Record confirmed.

The pardon — September 8, 1974, full pardon for Watergate-era crimes; no pre-arranged deal per Ford testimony; acceptance implies guilt per Burdick. Record confirmed.

When the president does it — Nixon to Frost, 1977; taught as patently false claim Nixon may have believed. Record: line confirmed.

69 charged / 48 convicted — Corrects the video 22-convicted figure. Record confirmed; carry the corrected totals.
