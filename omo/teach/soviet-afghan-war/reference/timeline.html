Timeline — as of chapter 3

Legend — colour carries the kind, text carries the status
Tag Meaning
politics and decision Regime changes, party events, and decisions taken in Moscow, Kabul or party congresses.
war and violence Uprisings, offensives, operations, assassinations, and the fighting itself.
diplomacy and agreement Treaties, accords, talks, resolutions, and constitutions.
outside powers Washington, Islamabad, Riyadh and the pipeline: findings, doctrines, sanctions, and aid.

Every entry also says documented (primary or strong multi-source support in the lesson's record box) or contested (serious accounts disagree, or the figure is an estimate) — in words, never in colour. Date spellings follow the lessons exactly.

Before 1978 — the buffer

  • Aug 1919
    Anglo-Afghan Treaty of Rawalpindi; independent Afghanistan, first recognised by Moscow (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • 28 Feb 1921
    Soviet-Afghan friendship treaty ratified (signed 1920): border closed to the Basmachi, friendship and arms against Britain (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • 1926 · 1931
    Neutrality and non-aggression treaties; Kabul's bi-tarafi doctrine (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • 1929
    Nadir Shah takes the throne (documented). politics and decision
  • 1933
    Zahir Shah becomes king (documented). politics and decision
  • May 1954
    Pakistan signs mutual defence agreement with the United States (documented). outside powers
  • Sep 1954
    Pakistan joins SEATO (documented). outside powers
  • 1955
    Daoud signs Soviet arms agreement through Czechoslovakia (documented). outside powers
  • Sep 1955
    Pakistan joins the Baghdad Pact, the future CENTO (documented). outside powers
  • 15–19 Dec 1955
    Bulganin and Khrushchev visit Kabul; large development credit extended, conventionally $100 million (documented via histories, second-tier — no primary ledger cited). outside powers
  • 1964
    Zahir Shah's constitution opens the parliamentary experiment (documented). politics and decision
  • 1 Jan 1965
    Founding congress of the PDPA; Taraki general secretary (documented). politics and decision
  • 1967
    PDPA splits into Khalq (Taraki, Amin) and Parcham (Karmal) (documented). politics and decision
  • 17 Jul 1973
    Daoud's nearly bloodless coup against Zahir Shah; republic declared (documented). politics and decision

1978–1979 — revolution, refusal, decision

  • 17 Apr 1978
    Murder of the Parchami Mir Akbar Khyber; Daoud orders PDPA arrests (documented). war and violence
  • 27–28 Apr 1978
    Saur revolution: ~250 tanks in Kabul, Daoud and family dead in the Arg, Taraki chairs the Revolutionary Council (documented). war and violence
  • Jul 1978
    Amin purges the Parchamis; Karmal posted to Prague, defects into Soviet shelter (documented). politics and decision
  • Autumn 1978
    Khalq revolutionary decrees: land, debt, bride-price, literacy (documented). politics and decision
  • 5 Dec 1978
    Twenty-year Treaty of Friendship with Brezhnev; Article 4, the later legal pretext (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • 1 Jan 1979
    Land reform drive rolls out; countryside reads war on Islam and custom (documented). politics and decision
  • Jan 1979
    The Shah deposed in Iran; Moscow fears US replacement bases and Islamic contagion (documented). outside powers
  • Feb 1979
    Ambassador Dubs kidnapped and killed in a botched rescue (documented). war and violence
  • 15 Mar 1979
    Herat uprising and 17th Division mutiny; city held days, retaken at great cost; Soviet death toll contested — official 2 vs press ~200, best estimate a handful (death toll contested). war and violence
  • 17–18 Mar 1979
    Full Politburo debates and refuses troops; Kosygin leads (documented). politics and decision
  • 18 Mar 1979
    Taraki begs Kosygin for disguised Soviet troops: "Uzbeks, Tajiks, and Turkmens in civilian clothing" (documented). politics and decision
  • 3 Jul 1979
    Carter finding authorises up to $695,000 in cash, non-military supplies and propaganda, via third countries (documented; whether meant to induce invasion contested). outside powers
  • Early Sep 1979
    Taraki embraced by Brezhnev in Moscow; advised to remove Amin (documented). politics and decision
  • 14 Sep 1979
    Palace shoot-out; Tarun killed, Amin escapes, Taraki arrested (documented). war and violence
  • 8 Oct 1979
    Taraki smothered on Amin's orders; Brezhnev's turning point (documented). war and violence
  • 27 Oct 1979
    Amin receives Archer Blood alone, 40 minutes, in English (documented). outside powers
  • 29 Oct 1979
    Andropov–Ustinov–Gromyko–Ponomarev commission: prevent Amin's "political re-orientation … towards the West" (documented). politics and decision
  • Early Dec 1979
    Andropov's handwritten memo to Brezhnev recommends Amin's removal (documented via Dobrynin's 1995 copy). politics and decision
  • 8 Dec 1979
    Andropov, Ustinov and Gromyko meet Brezhnev; assent obtained (documented). politics and decision
  • 12 Dec 1979
    One-page resolution "On the Situation in 'A'", handwritten by Chernenko, signed without debate; Kosygin absent sick (documented). politics and decision
  • 24 Dec 1979
    Ustinov signs the movement directive; 40th Army decision announced (documented). war and violence
  • 24–27 Dec 1979
    Airlift into Kabul and Bagram; 5th Guards Motor Rifle Division crosses from Termez (documented). war and violence
  • 25 Dec 1979
    40th Army begins crossing on the ground and in the air, ~30,000 initial combat strength (documented). war and violence
  • 26 Dec 1979
    Brzezinski's "arc of crisis" memo: "age-long dream of Moscow" for the Indian Ocean — documented as his claim, rejected as explanation. outside powers
  • 27 Dec 1979
    Operation Storm-333: Tajbeg Palace stormed, Amin killed; Karmal's pre-recorded statement over Radio Kabul (documented). war and violence
  • 28 Dec 1979
    Karmal in place in Kabul; published justification cites staged requests, Article 4 and UN Charter Article 51 (documented; requests treated as manufactured). politics and decision

1980–1985 — the unwinnable middle

  • Jan 1980
    Carter amends the finding: money then weapons through the ISI; programme toward $50 million; mujahideen called "freedom fighters" (documented). outside powers
  • Jan 1980
    Carter Doctrine declared; grain and technology embargo, SALT II shelved, fishing rights restricted (documented). outside powers
  • 14 Jan 1980
    UN General Assembly demands withdrawal, 104–18; renewed annually (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • 20 Jan 1980
    Carter's Olympic ultimatum on Meet the Press (documented). outside powers
  • Feb 1980
    Brzezinski secures Saudi dollar-for-dollar matching; Egyptian, Chinese and MI6 channels alongside (documented). outside powers
  • 21 Mar 1980
    US Olympic boycott announced (documented). outside powers
  • 1980
    Afghan army melted to ~25,000–30,000 from ~100,000–110,000; funding at $20–30 million a year (documented). war and violence
  • 1980–81
    Small Panjshir probes; the take–leave–return script (documented). war and violence
  • Jan 1981
    Reagan accedes; Cyclone becomes the centrepiece doctrine, funding rises steeply (documented). outside powers
  • Spring 1982
    12,000-man combined-arms Panjshir operation, 200+ fixed-wing sorties (documented). war and violence
  • Jun 1982
    Geneva proximity talks begin; Cordovez shuttling (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • Nov 1982
    Brezhnev dies; Andropov succeeds (documented). politics and decision
  • 1 Jan 1983
    Tkachev opens talks with Massoud (documented). war and violence
  • Jan 1983
    Massoud–Tkachev ceasefire; valley evacuated save Anava garrison (documented). war and violence
  • Apr 1983
    Cordovez calls the draft text "95 percent" complete; stalls on the post-withdrawal government (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • Feb 1984
    Andropov dies after fifteen months of hospital rule (documented). politics and decision
  • Feb 1984 – Mar 1985
    Chernenko interregnum; war runs on autopilot (documented). politics and decision
  • Apr–Sep 1984
    Panjshir VII: 15,000 men; mujahideen machine guns 13 to ~250 in two years (documented). war and violence
  • Dec 1984
    Ustinov dies (documented). politics and decision
  • 11 Mar 1985
    Gorbachev elected General Secretary (documented). politics and decision
  • 16 May 1985
    Seven Peshawar leaders announce the Islamic Unity alliance, joint communiqué (documented as the announcement; founding year 1981 vs 1985 contested). politics and decision

1985–1989 — the long exit

  • Apr 1985
    Politburo redefines the war as creating the conditions for leaving; Karmal told to widen his base (documented). politics and decision
  • 17 Oct 1985
    Decision in principle: citizens' letters read out; "arms only, not troops" (documented; no timetable yet). politics and decision
  • 17 Nov 1985
    Gorbachev warns Karmal troops out "by the summer of 1986" — pressure, not a plan (documented). politics and decision
  • 25 Feb – 6 Mar 1986
    27th CPSU Congress, Gorbachev's first as leader (documented). politics and decision
  • 26 Feb 1986
    "Bleeding wound"; withdrawal "in the nearest future" (documented). politics and decision
  • 4 May 1986
    Karmal resigns as PDPA General Secretary; Najibullah replaces him (documented). politics and decision
  • Sep 1986
    First Stingers reach mujahideen hands via the ISI (documented). outside powers
  • 25 Sep 1986
    First engagement near Jalalabad: three Mi-24s downed; ~2,000–2,500 supplied, ~269 claimed (documented engagement; decisiveness contested). war and violence
  • Nov 1986
    Karmal loses the Revolutionary Council chairmanship to Chamkani; exiled to Moscow (documented). politics and decision
  • 15 Jan 1987
    Najibullah offers a six-month ceasefire and a share in politics (documented). politics and decision
  • Jul 1987
    Ghor gathering under Ismail Khan rejects reconciliation; ceasefire lapses (documented). politics and decision
  • Autumn 1987
    Timetable set: military wanted two years, politicians imposed nine months (documented). politics and decision
  • Late 1987
    Loya Jirga ratifies the new constitution: name, Islam, parties on paper (documented; exact sitting date contested). diplomacy and agreement
  • 19 Nov 1987 – 10 Jan 1988
    Operation Magistral: Gromov and Tanai force the Gardez–Khost road; siege lifted (documented). war and violence
  • 7–8 Jan 1988
    39 men of the 9th Company hold Hill 3234 (documented). war and violence
  • 8 Feb 1988
    Gorbachev publicly announces the withdrawal decision (documented). politics and decision
  • 8 Apr 1988
    Last Geneva round ends; Cordovez announces every instrument finalised (documented). diplomacy and agreement
  • 14 Apr 1988
    Four Geneva instruments signed: non-interference, guarantees, refugee return, the clock (documented; mujahideen unsigned, pipeline uncut). diplomacy and agreement
  • 15 May 1988
    Accords enter into force; phase one begins (documented). war and violence
  • 15 Aug 1988
    Roughly half the force out, on schedule, UNGOMAP-verified (documented). war and violence
  • 15 Aug 1988 – 1 Jan 1989
    Pause: no significant withdrawal observed; resumption postponed "in the light of prevailing conditions" (documented). war and violence
  • Mid-Nov 1988
    Phase two begins (documented). war and violence
  • 23 Jan 1989
    Operation Typhoon begins against Massoud astride the Kabul–Salang road (documented). war and violence
  • 15 Feb 1989
    Gromov walks over the Friendship Bridge last; no Politburo member greets the army (documented). war and violence
The cost ledger (1989)

Soviet official count 14,453 dead (9,511 action + 2,386 wounds + 2,556 disease and accidents), 53,753 wounded — documented as the official count, contested as the true total (internal reporting implies 26,000+). Afghan dead: DRA army 58,000+ killed; mujahideen 75,000–90,000 (tentative estimate); civilians 560,000–2,000,000, ~1 million the common figure — all contested ranges. Soviet aid ~$3 billion a year at the end is Coll's estimate, never an audited total.
