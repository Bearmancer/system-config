Timeline — as of chapter 3

Covers chapters 1–3: August 1919 – 15 February 1989. Every dated entry from the three lessons, in one place. Cast map · Glossary · Chapter index

Before 1978 — the buffer

  • Aug 1919
    Anglo-Afghan Treaty of Rawalpindi; independent Afghanistan, first recognised by Moscow (documented).
  • 28 Feb 1921
    Soviet-Afghan friendship treaty ratified (signed 1920): border closed to the Basmachi, friendship and arms against Britain (documented).
  • 1926 · 1931
    Neutrality and non-aggression treaties; Kabul's bi-tarafi doctrine (documented).
  • 1929
    Nadir Shah takes the throne (documented).
  • 1933
    Zahir Shah becomes king (documented).
  • May 1954
    Pakistan signs mutual defence agreement with the United States (documented).
  • Sep 1954
    Pakistan joins SEATO (documented).
  • 1955
    Daoud signs Soviet arms agreement through Czechoslovakia (documented).
  • Sep 1955
    Pakistan joins the Baghdad Pact, the future CENTO (documented).
  • 15–19 Dec 1955
    Bulganin and Khrushchev visit Kabul; large development credit extended, conventionally $100 million (documented via histories, second-tier — no primary ledger cited).
  • 1964
    Zahir Shah's constitution opens the parliamentary experiment (documented).
  • 1 Jan 1965
    Founding congress of the PDPA; Taraki general secretary (documented).
  • 1967
    PDPA splits into Khalq (Taraki, Amin) and Parcham (Karmal) (documented).
  • 17 Jul 1973
    Daoud's nearly bloodless coup against Zahir Shah; republic declared (documented).

1978–1979 — revolution, refusal, decision

  • 17 Apr 1978
    Murder of the Parchami Mir Akbar Khyber; Daoud orders PDPA arrests (documented).
  • 27–28 Apr 1978
    Saur revolution: ~250 tanks in Kabul, Daoud and family dead in the Arg, Taraki chairs the Revolutionary Council (documented).
  • Jul 1978
    Amin purges the Parchamis; Karmal posted to Prague, defects into Soviet shelter (documented).
  • Autumn 1978
    Khalq revolutionary decrees: land, debt, bride-price, literacy (documented).
  • 5 Dec 1978
    Twenty-year Treaty of Friendship with Brezhnev; Article 4, the later legal pretext (documented).
  • 1 Jan 1979
    Land reform drive rolls out; countryside reads war on Islam and custom (documented).
  • Jan 1979
    The Shah deposed in Iran; Moscow fears US replacement bases and Islamic contagion (documented).
  • Feb 1979
    Ambassador Dubs kidnapped and killed in a botched rescue (documented).
  • 15 Mar 1979
    Herat uprising and 17th Division mutiny; city held days, retaken at great cost; Soviet death toll contested — official 2 vs press ~200, best estimate a handful (death toll contested).
  • 17–18 Mar 1979
    Full Politburo debates and refuses troops; Kosygin leads (documented).
  • 18 Mar 1979
    Taraki begs Kosygin for disguised Soviet troops: "Uzbeks, Tajiks, and Turkmens in civilian clothing" (documented).
  • 3 Jul 1979
    Carter finding authorises up to $695,000 in cash, non-military supplies and propaganda, via third countries (documented; whether meant to induce invasion contested).
  • Early Sep 1979
    Taraki embraced by Brezhnev in Moscow; advised to remove Amin (documented).
  • 14 Sep 1979
    Palace shoot-out; Tarun killed, Amin escapes, Taraki arrested (documented).
  • 8 Oct 1979
    Taraki smothered on Amin's orders; Brezhnev's turning point (documented).
  • 27 Oct 1979
    Amin receives Archer Blood alone, 40 minutes, in English (documented).
  • 29 Oct 1979
    Andropov–Ustinov–Gromyko–Ponomarev commission: prevent Amin's "political re-orientation … towards the West" (documented).
  • Early Dec 1979
    Andropov's handwritten memo to Brezhnev recommends Amin's removal (documented via Dobrynin's 1995 copy).
  • 8 Dec 1979
    Andropov, Ustinov and Gromyko meet Brezhnev; assent obtained (documented).
  • 12 Dec 1979
    One-page resolution "On the Situation in 'A'", handwritten by Chernenko, signed without debate; Kosygin absent sick (documented).
  • 24 Dec 1979
    Ustinov signs the movement directive; 40th Army decision announced (documented).
  • 24–27 Dec 1979
    Airlift into Kabul and Bagram; 5th Guards Motor Rifle Division crosses from Termez (documented).
  • 25 Dec 1979
    40th Army begins crossing on the ground and in the air, ~30,000 initial combat strength (documented).
  • 26 Dec 1979
    Brzezinski's "arc of crisis" memo: "age-long dream of Moscow" for the Indian Ocean — documented as his claim, rejected as explanation.
  • 27 Dec 1979
    Operation Storm-333: Tajbeg Palace stormed, Amin killed; Karmal's pre-recorded statement over Radio Kabul (documented).
  • 28 Dec 1979
    Karmal in place in Kabul; published justification cites staged requests, Article 4 and UN Charter Article 51 (documented; requests treated as manufactured).

1980–1985 — the unwinnable middle

  • Jan 1980
    Carter amends the finding: money then weapons through the ISI; programme toward $50 million; mujahideen called "freedom fighters" (documented).
  • Jan 1980
    Carter Doctrine declared; grain and technology embargo, SALT II shelved, fishing rights restricted (documented).
  • 14 Jan 1980
    UN General Assembly demands withdrawal, 104–18; renewed annually (documented).
  • 20 Jan 1980
    Carter's Olympic ultimatum on Meet the Press (documented).
  • Feb 1980
    Brzezinski secures Saudi dollar-for-dollar matching; Egyptian, Chinese and MI6 channels alongside (documented).
  • 21 Mar 1980
    US Olympic boycott announced (documented).
  • 1980
    Afghan army melted to ~25,000–30,000 from ~100,000–110,000; funding at $20–30 million a year (documented).
  • 1980–81
    Small Panjshir probes; the take–leave–return script (documented).
  • Jan 1981
    Reagan accedes; Cyclone becomes the centrepiece doctrine, funding rises steeply (documented).
  • Spring 1982
    12,000-man combined-arms Panjshir operation, 200+ fixed-wing sorties (documented).
  • Jun 1982
    Geneva proximity talks begin; Cordovez shuttling (documented).
  • Nov 1982
    Brezhnev dies; Andropov succeeds (documented).
  • 1 Jan 1983
    Tkachev opens talks with Massoud (documented).
  • Jan 1983
    Massoud–Tkachev ceasefire; valley evacuated save Anava garrison (documented).
  • Apr 1983
    Cordovez calls the draft text "95 percent" complete; stalls on the post-withdrawal government (documented).
  • Feb 1984
    Andropov dies after fifteen months of hospital rule (documented).
  • Feb 1984 – Mar 1985
    Chernenko interregnum; war runs on autopilot (documented).
  • Apr–Sep 1984
    Panjshir VII: 15,000 men; mujahideen machine guns 13 to ~250 in two years (documented).
  • Dec 1984
    Ustinov dies (documented).
  • 11 Mar 1985
    Gorbachev elected General Secretary (documented).
  • 16 May 1985
    Seven Peshawar leaders announce the Islamic Unity alliance, joint communiqué (documented as the announcement; founding year 1981 vs 1985 contested).

1985–1989 — the long exit

  • Apr 1985
    Politburo redefines the war as creating the conditions for leaving; Karmal told to widen his base (documented).
  • 17 Oct 1985
    Decision in principle: citizens' letters read out; "arms only, not troops" (documented; no timetable yet).
  • 17 Nov 1985
    Gorbachev warns Karmal troops out "by the summer of 1986" — pressure, not a plan (documented).
  • 25 Feb – 6 Mar 1986
    27th CPSU Congress, Gorbachev's first as leader (documented).
  • 26 Feb 1986
    "Bleeding wound"; withdrawal "in the nearest future" (documented).
  • 4 May 1986
    Karmal resigns as PDPA General Secretary; Najibullah replaces him (documented).
  • Sep 1986
    First Stingers reach mujahideen hands via the ISI (documented).
  • 25 Sep 1986
    First engagement near Jalalabad: three Mi-24s downed; ~2,000–2,500 supplied, ~269 claimed (documented engagement; decisiveness contested).
  • Nov 1986
    Karmal loses the Revolutionary Council chairmanship to Chamkani; exiled to Moscow (documented).
  • 15 Jan 1987
    Najibullah offers a six-month ceasefire and a share in politics (documented).
  • Jul 1987
    Ghor gathering under Ismail Khan rejects reconciliation; ceasefire lapses (documented).
  • Autumn 1987
    Timetable set: military wanted two years, politicians imposed nine months (documented).
  • Late 1987
    Loya Jirga ratifies the new constitution: name, Islam, parties on paper (documented; exact sitting date contested).
  • 19 Nov 1987 – 10 Jan 1988
    Operation Magistral: Gromov and Tanai force the Gardez–Khost road; siege lifted (documented).
  • 7–8 Jan 1988
    39 men of the 9th Company hold Hill 3234 (documented).
  • 8 Feb 1988
    Gorbachev publicly announces the withdrawal decision (documented).
  • 8 Apr 1988
    Last Geneva round ends; Cordovez announces every instrument finalised (documented).
  • 14 Apr 1988
    Four Geneva instruments signed: non-interference, guarantees, refugee return, the clock (documented; mujahideen unsigned, pipeline uncut).
  • 15 May 1988
    Accords enter into force; phase one begins (documented).
  • 15 Aug 1988
    Roughly half the force out, on schedule, UNGOMAP-verified (documented).
  • 15 Aug 1988 – 1 Jan 1989
    Pause: no significant withdrawal observed; resumption postponed "in the light of prevailing conditions" (documented).
  • Mid-Nov 1988
    Phase two begins (documented).
  • 23 Jan 1989
    Operation Typhoon begins against Massoud astride the Kabul–Salang road (documented).
  • 15 Feb 1989
    Gromov walks over the Friendship Bridge last; no Politburo member greets the army (documented).
The cost ledger (1989)

Soviet official count 14,453 dead (9,511 action + 2,386 wounds + 2,556 disease and accidents), 53,753 wounded — documented as the official count, contested as the true total (internal reporting implies 26,000+). Afghan dead: DRA army 58,000+ killed; mujahideen 75,000–90,000 (tentative estimate); civilians 560,000–2,000,000, ~1 million the common figure — all contested ranges. Soviet aid ~$3 billion a year at the end is Coll's estimate, never an audited total.
