# Resources — Soviet-Afghan War

Tiers: `[D]` documents/records · `[B]` books/memoirs · `[S]` scholarship/press · `[R]` reference surfaces · `[W]` wisdom/communities.
Every entry is annotated with what it is good for and its cautions. Advocacy or interested sources are labelled. Reference surfaces are never sole support.

This course has **no single source**. It is built from the documentary record, so this list is the course's entire evidentiary base. Consolidated from the chapters' own citation lists at the end of the build pass.

## [D] Documents and records

- **NSA (National Security Archive) — "The Soviet Invasion of Afghanistan, 1979" briefing book (29 January 2019).** `https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/afghanistan-russia-programs/2019-01-29/soviet-invasion-afghanistan-1979-not-trumps-terrorists-nor-zbigs-warm-water-ports` — the core of chapter 1: the March 1979 refusal, the October–December decision chain, the 8 and 12 December meetings, and the treatment of the Afghan "invitation" as legal cover. Use for: who said what in the room, and when.
- **NSA — "Afghanistan and the Soviet Withdrawal 1989 — 20 Years Later".** `https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/legacy-posting/afghanistan-soviet-withdrawal-1989-20-years-later` — primary documents for the withdrawal: the "bleeding wound" usage, the October 1985 decision in principle, the 1988–89 timetable. Use for: Gorbachev's own framing.
- **Wilson Center — Cold War International History Project digital archive, Soviet invasion of Afghanistan collection.** `https://digitalarchive.wilsoncenter.org/collection/76/soviet-invasion-of-afghanistan` — translated Soviet, Afghan, Polish and East German documents. Use for: the Afghan side and bloc reactions.
- **US State Department — _Foreign Relations of the United States_, 1977–1980, Volume XII: Afghanistan.** `https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v12` — US policy chain. Specific documents used: the December 1978 treaty (doc 33), the fog around Taraki's death (doc 75), and the Blood/Amstutz cables. The volume's document pages are individually numbered and can be deep-linked.
- **US State Department — Office of the Historian milestones, "The Soviet Invasion of Afghanistan and the US Response".** `https://history.state.gov/milestones/1977-1980/soviet-invasion-afghanistan` — narrative orientation for US policy; a government account, so use as a guide to the documents rather than as a finding.
- **UN — UNGOMAP background and mandate.** `https://peacekeeping.un.org/sites/default/files/past/ungomap/background.html` — the observer mission set up by the Geneva Accords.
- **Geneva Agreements on Afghanistan, 14 April 1988 — full text.** `https://www.dipublico.org/100569/geneva-agreements-on-afghanistan` — the four instruments; essential for chapter 3's central point about what the accords did and did not bind.
- **US DTIC — _Into the Breach: Soviet Military Operations in Afghanistan, 1980–1982_ (ADA547558)** `https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA547558.pdf` and companion report ADA551037 `https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA551037.pdf` — US military analytical studies of Soviet operational practice. Use for: the 29 February 1980 order for "active operations", force structure, and limited-aims doctrine. An interested analysis, but document-rich.
- **2001–2009.state.gov — the Carter Doctrine page.** `https://2001-2009.state.gov/r/pa/ho/time/qfp/104481.htm` — the 23 January 1980 declaration in its own words.

## [B] Books and memoirs

- **Artemy Kalinovsky, _A Long Goodbye: The Soviet Withdrawal from Afghanistan_ (Harvard, 2011)** — the standard scholarly account of the exit and the best single spine for chapter 3. Identifies the men who argued against the timetable and why.
- **Rodric Braithwaite, _Afgantsy: The Russians in Afghanistan, 1979–89_ (2011)** — British ambassador in Moscow; strongest on the Soviet experience of the war and on how it was seen at home.
- **Diego Cordovez & Selig Harrison, _Out of Afghanistan_ (1995)** — the Geneva negotiation from a participant-historian. Clearly interested in Cordovez's own role.
- **Steve Coll, _Ghost Wars_ (2004)** — the US, Saudi and Pakistani pipeline; prize-winning journalism. Careful with sourcing, and note that some single-year Cyclone figures remain approximate because the ledgers are classified.
- **Mohammed Yousaf & Mark Adkin, _The Bear Trap_ (1992)** — ISI's own account. Openly interested; valuable precisely as an interested account of the conduit.
- **Aleksandr Lyakhovsky, _The Tragedy and Valor of the Afghani_ (1995)** — Soviet general staff–adjacent history; the source of much of the Soviet order-of-battle detail.
- **Mark Galeotti, _Storm-333: KGB and Spetsnaz seize Kabul, Christmas 1979_ (2021)** — the assault itself, in detail.
- **V. Kornienko, _The Cold War: Testimony of a Participant_ (1994)**; **A. Aleksandrov-Agentov**; **V. Varennikov's** testimony — memoir testimony by men in the room. Treat all memoir recollection as interested testimony and date it against the documents.
- **Thomas Barfield, _Afghanistan: A Cultural and Political History_ (2010)** — the long-run Afghan social structure the war ran into.

## [S] Scholarship and press

- **Afghanistan Analysts Network — "Failings of Inclusivity: the Herat uprising of March 1979".** `https://www.afghanistan-analysts.org/en/reports/context-culture/failings-of-inclusivity-the-herat-uprising-of-march-1979` — the best free treatment of Herat, including why the death toll is contested.
- **RFE/RL — "Poisonings, Assassination, and a Coup: the Secret Soviet Invasion of Afghanistan" (December 2019).** `https://www.rferl.org/a/poisonings-assassination-and-a-coup-the-secret-soviet-invasion-of-afghanistan/30347141.html` — the Storm-333 execution sequence, including the earlier poisoning attempt.
- **RFE/RL — "That time an Afghan president-to-be was secretly hiding in a Czechoslovak forest".** `https://www.rferl.org/a/that-time-an-afghan-president-to-be-was-secretly-hiding-in-a-czechoslovak-forest/30250494.html` — Karmal's exile and the Soviet handling of it.
- **VOA — "How Carter's covert aid to Afghan rebels redefined his foreign policy record".** `https://www.voanews.com/a/how-carter-s-covert-aid-to-afghan-rebels-redefined-his-foreign-policy-record/7926876.html` — the 3 July 1979 finding. US government–funded broadcaster: note the institutional interest.
- **Human Rights Watch, _Afghanistan_ report (2005), chapter 3.** `https://www.hrw.org/reports/2005/afghanistan0605/3.htm` — civilian harm and displacement.
- **History.com — Carter announces the Olympic boycott.** `https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/march-21/carter-announces-olympic-boycott` — press-grade reference for the boycott; not sole support for anything.
- **Brzezinski's 1998 _Nouvel Observateur_ interview** (mirror used: `https://dgibbs.arizona.edu/content/brzezinski-interview-2`) — the origin of the "we induced them" claim. **A contested claim, not a finding**: the interview was given 19 years after the fact and is contested by, among others, Conor Tobin in _Diplomatic History_ 44:2 (2020). The course quotes it as Brzezinski's own characterisation.
- **Journals of record for the field:** _Journal of Cold War Studies_, _Cold War History_, _Central Asian Survey_, _Diplomatic History_.

- **Encyclopaedia Iranica** entries on Afghanistan; **Library of Congress country studies**; standard encyclopaedic chronologies — used only for orientation and to cross-check spellings. Never sole support for any claim in this course.

## Gaps

Unfindable or contested items carried visibly in the chapters; recorded here so nothing silently disappears:

1. **Herat, March 1979 — death toll.** Contested; figures up to 25,000 circulate without a documentary base. The chapter states the range and its uncertainty.
2. **Soviet citizens killed at Herat, March 1979 — exact number unfindable.**
3. **14 September 1979 palace shootout — who fired first.** Unfindable in the record; the chapter says so.
4. **Whether Hafizullah Amin genuinely reoriented toward Washington or was hedging.** Contested; both readings are attributed.
5. **Brzezinski's "Afghan trap" claim.** Contested, with origin and named critics given in chapter 1.
6. **Cumulative Soviet dead by March 1985.** No reliable annual series located; only the later Krivosheev totals and Western yearly estimates. The chapter states this rather than manufacturing a figure.
7. **Monthly Afghan army strengths, 1980–85.** Unfindable; only wide bands are documented.
8. **Single-year Operation Cyclone funding figures.** Approximate — the ledgers remain classified.
9. **Massoud's January 1983 truce duration.** Contested (six months vs one year).
10. **Panjshir mujahideen losses.** Contested across sources.
11. **Cordovez's claim about a 1983 near-decision to withdraw.** Contested by other accounts of the same talks.
12. **Chapter 3's citation base is thinner than chapters 1 and 2** — the external links recovered from its page number three, against a book-based apparatus named in its text. If the user wants chapter 3 deepened, the first target is a fuller document-level sourcing pass on the withdrawal decisions.
