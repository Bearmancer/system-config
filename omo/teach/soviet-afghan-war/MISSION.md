# Soviet-Afghan War: how Moscow got in, and why it took ten years

> **Status: PROVISIONAL.** Drafted from the first request — _"Explain how Soviet is connected to Afghanistan, or why they warred for ten years, because it seems confusing how Russia suddenly decided Afghanistan."_ Confirm or correct at the next check-in; a wrong mission mis-steers every future chapter.

## Why

The user is confused by an apparent discontinuity. The USSR and Afghanistan had decades of ordinary, mostly quiet relations — then in December 1979 Moscow sent an army across the border, and then did not leave for ten years. The puzzle is the _seam_: how an invasion that looked sudden from outside was not sudden inside the machinery that ordered it, and why the leaving took four years after the decision to leave was taken.

Working hypothesis: the user wants the **mechanism**, not military trivia — how a superpower talks itself into a war on its own frontier and then cannot extract itself. Same habit as the other teach workspaces: keep what the participants _claimed_ visibly separate from what the _record_ shows.

## Success looks like

- Can retell the chain from the 1921 Soviet-Afghan treaty to Storm-333 (27 December 1979) without a gap where "suddenly" used to be.
- Can name the decision-makers, the Afghan clients, the outside patrons and the commanders, and say what each of them wanted.
- Can explain the decade in terms of mechanism — sanctuary, terrain, patronage, doctrine, cost, alliance politics — rather than adjectives.
- Can say which claims are documented, which are contested between historians, and which remain unfindable.

## Constraints

- **No single source.** This course is built from the documentary record (Politburo transcripts, declassified US and Soviet documents, memoirs, scholarship), with citations on every load-bearing claim. There is no "the video says / the record shows" split here in the usual sense; the replacement discipline is **documented fact vs contested reading**, kept visibly separate in every chapter.
- Learning from a written course, not a chaptered video — chapters are short, self-contained, completable in one sitting.
- Chat terse; workspace artifacts written in full prose.
- Method transparency expected: the user asks _how_ something was derived, not just the result. Every chapter carries a method box.
- Optionality expected: every chapter says what matters and what can be skipped.

## Out of scope

- The Afghan civil war after the Soviet withdrawal (1992 onward) beyond a short, clearly-marked coda.
- Order-of-battle detail and weapon-by-weapon military history for its own sake.
- Present-day Afghan politics.
